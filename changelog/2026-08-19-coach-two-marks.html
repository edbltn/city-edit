Six notes on the coach, and they are one change: the cast step stops being a single box asking a closed question and becomes TWO marks — the question, and the fact that its answer is still yours to change. The marks are horizontal now, sitting beside their controls with the pointer on a side ([ bla bla bla ]▶) rather than underneath them. The vote-type picker is exempt from the greying, because the second mark is pointing at it and saying “change your vote if you need to” — and that exemption was checked all the way through to elementFromPoint, since a control that LOOKS available and swallows the click is worse than one honestly greyed out. The question reads the live selection, so changing the type in that dropdown changes the question. Neither mark leaves until the vote is cast. And each is told to keep off not just the other mark’s box but the other mark’s CONTROL — which at 390px is the difference between a working invitation and a callout sitting on top of the thing it just told you to press.

SHAPE

Horizontal: a label beside the control, not a caption under it

Symptom

The marks were boxes under their controls with the pointer on a horizontal edge. The owner wants the pointer on a SIDE, aimed across at what it describes — [ bla bla bla ]▶.

Root cause

  • The first version placed below by default because every anchor is in the top bar and below is where the map is. That is a good reason to put a box somewhere and no reason at all for what it should LOOK like
  • A box under a control reads as a caption on it; a box beside it with a tip touching it reads as a label. Labelling is what moving off the bottom strip was for in the first place

What changed

  • The tip now grows out of a VERTICAL edge and the box sits beside its control, left by default. The four sides are named for where the BOX sits, so left means the box is left of the anchor with its tip on its own right edge
  • The tip slides DOWN the box's edge to stay on the control when the box is clamped — the same rule as before, now on the other axis. One custom property, --coach-arrow, read from the left for a vertical tip and from the top for a horizontal one
  • Where nothing legible fits either side it goes back under. At 390px the Start/End plate is nearly the whole screen: 9px of room to its left. A 40px-wide callout obeying the shape rule is worse than a readable one below it, so under a floor of 168px the tip moves to a horizontal edge — the box stays wide and short either way
  • Which orientation is in play is decided from the ANCHOR AND VIEWPORT ALONE, never from the box's own size, so the caller can ask before the box exists and apply it as a max-width. Asking the other way round is circular: the width decides the wrapping, which decides the height, which decides where a vertical box can go

THE CAST STEP

Two marks, and nothing takes them down but the vote

Symptom

One box asked a closed question naming a vote type, and a first-timer who disagreed with that type had no way of knowing the picker above it was theirs to move.

Root cause

  • The line used to end “— or pick another above”, which is the same instruction with nothing to look at. It was dropped when the chrome started greying, because an instruction to use a faded control contradicts the screen
  • Nothing else on the screen said the answer was still open, and the greying actively said the opposite

What changed

  • Two marks at once. The left one points at the vote-type picker and reads “Change your vote if you need to”; the right one asks the question. One ×, on the mark carrying the question — two closing controls read as two separate things to deal with
  • Neither leaves until the vote is cast. The address-search stand-down is now scoped to start and end, which are the only steps whose own anchor turns into a search box. At cast the fields are not the subject and the rule is that the marks stay
  • They take opposite sides by construction — the picker's mark goes left, the pair's goes right — which is also why each gets the width of the side it will actually use. Flipping after measuring would leave a box sized for the roomier side trying to fit in the narrower one

THE INVITATION

The picker is exempt from the greying — and actually usable

Symptom

The greying faded every control except the step's own target. A mark telling somebody to use the vote-type picker, over a faded and possibly click-swallowing vote-type picker, is worse than not offering.

Root cause

  • “Undimmed” and “interactive” are different claims, and only one of them is visible. A control that looks available and is not is a worse failure than one that is honestly greyed out
  • There are three separate ways this could have been broken: a scrim in front of it, a pointer-events rule, or a menu opening underneath something

What changed

  • The lit rule now covers [data-coach="votetype"] as well as the cast pair, for the cast step only
  • Checked all three ways, not assumed: the scrim is calc(var(--z-chrome) - 1) so it was never in front of the bar; nothing sets pointer-events on it; and the menu it opens is --z-dropdown, higher again
  • Verified by hit-testing — elementFromPoint at the picker's centre returns the picker's own input, at 1200px and at 390px — and by opening the menu with a real click, which produced all 13 rows at z-index 700 with both marks still on screen
  • And belt-and-braces on the coach's own side: the callout box is pointer-events: none except its buttons, the rule MapNotice was built on. A coach must never eat a click meant for the app

THE OTHER HALF

Inviting somebody to change the vote type, and then showing them the old one, would be worse than never offering.

What changed

GEOMETRY

Keeping off the other mark — and off its control

Symptom

Two marks whose controls are neighbours both take their preferred side and overlap. Worse: at 390px the box on the −/+ pair lands squarely on the picker.

Root cause

  • The picker and the −/+ pair are adjacent columns on a desktop bar and stacked rows on a phone. Measured at 1200px they are 621–791 and 804–876; at 390px they are 8–311 on one row and 320–381 on the row above
  • Two overlapping callouts read as one broken one — and a callout covering the control it has just told you to press is the same failure as greying it out, only harder to notice

What changed

  • Each mark is told what to keep off: the other mark's BOX (read live from its element, because that box moves on every resize too) and the other mark's CONTROL
  • Sides are tried in order — preferred, its opposite, below, above — and the first that clears every blocker wins. At 390px that puts the pair's mark ABOVE the pair, clear of the picker below it
  • Trying sides rather than NUDGING is the point: a nudge moves the box off its own control and the tip has to stretch or lie, whereas every candidate in that list is still a box pointing straight at its anchor
  • If nothing clears, the preferred placement stands and the code says so. An overlap is better than a box in the wrong place entirely, and a stated last resort is better than a silent fifth fallback
