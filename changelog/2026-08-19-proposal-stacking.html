Stacking: pins before words, and latitude before votes

2026-08-19 · branch main

Two requests about paint order that turned out to live in two different renderers. The words beside a top proposal are MapLibre symbols on the GL canvas; the pins are Leaflet DOM above it, so “text on top of icons” is not a z-index anyone can write — and both ways to make it one (labels out of GL, or pins into it) cost more than the bug. So the text yields instead: every pin’s footprint is now placed before a single word is, and no claim is drawn under a pin at any zoom. The second request needed the opposite move — Leaflet already stacks markers by latitude, and we were burying that under each proposal’s vote count. Taking the count off hands the order back to geography — and then, once it was, a second constant showed through underneath it: corridors sat in bands of their own above every point pin, so latitude worked inside each kind and not between them. Both kinds now share one scale.

THE DECISION

Two renderers, and why neither one moved

Symptom

The ask was for the text under a top-proposal pin to draw ON TOP of other icons. It cannot, and no z-index anywhere in the app can make it.

Root cause

  • The words are MapLibre symbols, drawn on the GL canvas. The pins are Leaflet divIcons, drawn in a marker pane above that entire canvas — MapLibreBackground renders as a sibling before MapContainer. A pin therefore paints over GL text no matter how the GL style is ordered, and inside GL the proposal text is already on top of everything.
  • The z-index ladder cannot help either, and correctly so: #map and .leaflet-container each open a stacking context on purpose, which is the box that keeps Leaflet's six-figure marker offsets from painting over the topbar. Nothing inside the map can outrank anything outside it, and nothing outside it can reach in.

What changed

  • Rejected: move the labels into Leaflet. That trades away MapLibre's global collision index — the single reason the words are GL symbols. It is what makes proposal text push POI names aside, keeps labels off each other, and thins a dense downtown into pins instead of a wall of type. We would be hand-rolling decluttering to win a paint order.
  • Rejected: move the pins into MapLibre. Correct in the abstract — one engine, one collision index, one z-order — and a rewrite of drag, hover, the [×] badge, the cluster fan-out and the mid-drag gesture against symbols that are not DOM nodes. Far beyond a stacking fix.
  • Chosen: make the text yield instead of cover. The outcome the owner wants is that words are never buried under an icon; overlay is one way to get there and avoidance is the other, and only avoidance is reachable from one renderer. It also protects the rule that the pins stay the loudest thing on the map: text painted OVER pins would inverts exactly the hierarchy the labels are muted to preserve.

LABELS

One collision pass where there should have been two

Symptom

A neighbouring pin sat on a proposal’s words: MORE TREE|S with its last letter under a wheelchair pin, BETTER LIGHTI|NG under a pedestrian-signal pin. Measured on nyc-walkways at midtown: 5 of 10 labels struck at z12, 5 of 14 at z13, 2 of 15 at z14.

Root cause

  • Each proposal was ONE symbol carrying both the transparent pin puck and the words. Symbols are placed in rank order, so proposal #1’s words were placed, and then proposal #2’s puck was placed on top of them.
  • A puck is icon-allow-overlap and cannot be asked to stand down — Leaflet is going to draw that pin whatever MapLibre decides — so the icon of a later symbol always wins against the text of an earlier one. The collision index has no notion of ‘go back and move that label’.
  • The reveal schedule made it worse at the wide zooms: a proposal whose rank had not unlocked its label yet was filtered out of the layer entirely, so its pin reserved nothing while still being drawn.

What changed

  • The pucks move to their own layer above the words. Placement runs top-down, so every pin — labelled or not, at every zoom — is in the collision index before the first word is placed. A label that cannot clear the pins now stands down instead of being drawn and buried.
  • The puck reserves the pin’s ink (33px, grown by the 1.1× hover scale it pivots through) rather than its 42px element box, whose bottom 6px are empty. That matters now in a way it did not before: the puck is placed BEFORE the label it belongs to, so it can suppress its own label, and the old box left about a pixel of margin.
  • A test asserts that clearance across the whole zoom range, because the failure is silent: if the two boxes ever touch, every proposal label vanishes at that zoom with nothing but a non-fatal warning.

PINS

Latitude was already there; a vote count was standing on it

Symptom

Overlapping pins did not stack by latitude, which Leaflet does by default. Something was overriding it.

Root cause

  • Leaflet sets zIndex = layerPoint.y + zIndexOffset, and y grows southward, so the southern (nearer) pin paints on top for free.
  • Our offset was a state band PLUS the proposal’s own net votes (capped at 50k) or a corridor’s score (capped at 48k). y only reaches a few hundred among pins that can actually overlap, so a term in the tens of thousands did not merely outrank latitude — it erased it. Measured live: the corridor diamonds’ offsets ran 300200 to 300309, up to 109 pixels of ‘score lift’.

What changed

  • The in-band term is gone; the bands stay. Bands encode a JOB — fanned out, matched waypoint, open modal, covered corridor — and a pin doing a job must not be buried by one that is merely present.
  • The bands are 100k apart, so the only way y could carry a pin across a band is two pins 100,000 pixels apart, which cannot overlap and so cannot be seen to invert. That is asserted as a test, not assumed.
  • Ranking by votes was defensible on its own, but it is not the rule asked for and not one the eye can read: nothing on the map says which of two overlapping pins has more votes, so the stacking simply looked arbitrary.
  • The bands move out of two inline expressions into pinStacking.ts, where the regression itself — that no per-proposal quantity is added to an offset — is a test.

PINS · REFINEMENT

The same bug, one level up: a band per KIND

Symptom

With the vote count gone, latitude ordered the pins correctly WITHIN each kind and not at all between them — a corridor diamond always drew in front of a point square, however far north it was.

Root cause

  • Not panes, not layer groups, and not the GL/Leaflet split: both kinds are plain Leaflet markers in the same pane. The corridors simply carried bands of their own — 300000 uncovered, 400000 covered — above every band a point pin could reach.
  • That is the same failure as the vote count, one level up. A constant of 300,000 against a y that reaches a few hundred does not tilt the comparison, it deletes it: every member of the upper group beats every member of the lower one at any latitude.

What changed

  • The corridor bands are gone. There is now ONE function, pinZIndexOffset, called by both marker memos — and it takes no argument saying which kind it is looking at, so there is nowhere for a per-kind lift to come back.
  • The bands that remain are all states either kind can be in. covered (the corridor the selection is acting on) now shares a band with selected (the point pin holding the open modal), because that is one statement about a pin, not two.
  • This deliberately drops the old rule that “a corridor is never buried under point pins”. That rule IS the reported bug: the two kinds are peers on the map, so they are peers in the stack.
  • Both kinds anchor on their tail tip, so screen y is the same measurement for a square and a diamond — which is what makes one shared scale legitimate rather than merely convenient.

What was run

  • The cost, stated plainly. Labels that cannot clear the pins no longer draw: total labels at z13 went 14 → 5 and at z12 10 → 3. At the default zoom (14) the composition improves outright — the two mangled labels are gone and FIX CURB CUT, previously silenced by a MORE TREE|S that was drawn across a pin, now speaks. If the wide zooms want more words back, the knob is proposalRevealZoom, already documented as deliberately generous for exactly this reason.
  • No self-suppression — the silent catastrophe. A proposal centred in the viewport draws its label at every zoom from 11 through 21 on nyc-proposals, with its own puck present in the same frame.
  • Latitude ordering, within a kind: every proposal marker’s zIndex minus its screen y is exactly its band constant — i.e. no per-proposal term survives. Of 31 overlapping pin pairs across z12–z16, the southern one is on top in 31. The 5 remaining ties are pins at identical coordinates, which is what the cluster fan-out exists to disambiguate.
  • Latitude ordering, ACROSS the kinds, tested by construction rather than by inspection — a real square and a real diamond moved next to each other in the running app. Point 18px south: point in front (z 1591 vs 1573). Latitudes swapped: corridor in front (1591 vs 1573). Both arrangements at z13, z15 and z17, and confirmed by eye at z16 — the wheelchair square over the bike diamond, then the bike diamond over the wheelchair square.

For you

Manual checklist

  • Open http://localhost:3000/m/nyc-walkways at the default zoom and read the labels in midtown — every word should be whole. Before this, MORE TREES lost its last letter to the wheelchair pin beside it.
  • Zoom out to z12–z13 over Manhattan. Expect FEWER labels than before and none of them crossed by a pin; if that reads as too quiet, say so — the reveal schedule is the knob, and it is one line.
  • Zoom all the way in on a single pin (z19–z21). Its label must still draw directly under it; if labels vanish at a particular zoom, that is the self-collision failure and it is the one to report loudest.
  • Hover a pin so it grows 1.1×, and confirm its own label is still clear underneath.
  • Find two proposal pins that overlap and check the SOUTHERN one is on top — then drag the map so their screen positions swap sides and confirm the order follows.
  • Do that again with a square and a diamond — a point proposal and a corridor. That is the pairing that was broken: the diamond used to win every time. Now the southern one wins, whichever kind it is.
  • Confirm the interaction bands still win: open a proposal (its pin lifts), match a waypoint to one (the [×] badge must stay visible over any pin overlapping it), and explode a crowded cluster (the fanned group sits above everything).
