Isometric Grid

1 design 2 colourways 300 dpi built from tools/merch

The logo's grid read as a crossword, and it was never meant to. It's a street plan. So it's built as one: the same six-by-four cells lifted into isometric, every cell a volume — plain cells low-rise, the logo's crossed cells vacant lots, and the letter cells plinths with the letter standing on them. Flatten it back and it is still the wordmark.

How it's drawn

And what it took to get here

The letters stand up rather than lie down. Two earlier passes lay them flat on the roofs, and both failed the same way — on the ground plane a glyph shears in both axes at once, so it reads as a smudge at chest scale. Stood up in the wall plane only the baseline slants; every vertical stem stays vertical, and the wordmark survives across a room.

30° isometric, because it keeps the vertical axis vertical. That one property makes everything else cheap: a plane of constant height maps through a plain affine transform, and extruding a letterform is a pure screen translation — so the solid body is the front face stamped back up the page, not a solved silhouette.

Fills are opaque, not stacked opacity. Drawn with transparency every block lets the one behind ghost through; on cloth it's worse, since overlapping half-tones print as compounding ink rather than the tone you asked for. Each face is pre-composited against the garment instead — which is also why a colourway is tied to its blank, and the black files will read off on navy or heather.

The city is quiet on purpose. Blocks top out at half ink, the letters take all of it. Physically the light would be more even than that, but hierarchy beats accuracy on a garment.

Plinths clear the low-rise. Letter cells sit higher than the tallest plain block, so nothing in front can crop a letter at the knee — and that's what lets every block draw first and every letter draw after, which is what killed the notches where a letterform extruded into space its neighbour also claimed.

Getting it made

Seven steps, in order

Use Fourthwall: $0/month, and it acts as merchant of record, so it registers, collects and remits sales tax and VAT rather than leaving that to you. Printful + Shopify is $39/month before a single sale and hands you the filing.

  1. Build the files

    cd tools/merch && ./env/bin/python build_merch.py --lookbook --clean. Deterministic — the same command always produces the same bytes.

  2. Open the shop, pick the blank

    A free Fourthwall shop and a black tee. Match the blank to GARMENT_BLACK (#17171a) — the fills are composited against it.

  3. Upload, fit to print area

    The canvas is already the right physical size. Leave the art transparent: the streets between blocks are the shirt, and flattening it onto a black rectangle prints a black box on a black shirt at triple the ink.

  4. Order a sample

    The step to not skip. Whether five flat greys stay five greys through a white underbase is not a thing a screen can tell you.

    Check on arrival

    Block faces still separating into distinct tones · letter edges crisp, no banding down the extrusions · the darkest face still reading as ink rather than as shirt.

  5. Price from the sample

    ~$32 is roughly 2.5× base and in line with other civic-project shops.

  6. Point shop.cityedit.org at the store

    A CNAME at the registrar, then connect the subdomain in Fourthwall. One DNS record, zero deploys.

    Do not add it to terraform

    custom_domains is for hostnames Cloud Run answers, and a Cloud Run mapping needs DNS pointed at Google — the opposite of what this needs.

  7. Turn on the nav link

    Set SHOP_URL in NavRail.tsx and deploy. The tote glyph stays hidden while it's empty, so the link can't ship ahead of the store.
